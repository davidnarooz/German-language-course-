# Start lesson: German study guide and practice

A beginner-friendly companion to **Miteinander in Österreich A1.1, Start**. This guide covers greetings and farewells, introducing yourself, the alphabet and spelling, countries and origins, numbers 0–20, and colours. The book screenshots are in [course-book-screenshots/start](../course-book-screenshots/start).

## Learning goals

By the end, you can greet someone, say and spell your name, say where you come from, understand and use numbers 0–20, and name common colours.

## 1. Greetings and farewells

| German | English | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Guten Morgen! | Good morning! | Morning; polite or neutral |
| Guten Tag! | Good day / hello! | Daytime; polite or neutral |
| Grüß Gott! | Hello! | Common polite greeting in Austria and southern Germany |
| Guten Abend! | Good evening! | Evening |
| Hallo! | Hello! | Common and informal |
| Servus! | Hi! / Bye! | Informal, common in Austria |
| Auf Wiedersehen! | Goodbye! | Polite |
| Auf Wiederschauen! | Goodbye! | Polite, common in Austria |
| Tschüss! | Bye! | Informal |
| Baba! | Bye! | Informal, common in Austria |
| Gute Nacht! | Good night! | When going to bed or leaving late |

**Example**

> A: Guten Morgen!  
> B: Guten Morgen!  
> A: Auf Wiedersehen!  
> B: Auf Wiedersehen!

## 2. Introduce yourself

- **Ich bin Anna.** — I am Anna.
- **Mein Name ist Anna.** — My name is Anna.
- **Wie heißen Sie?** — What is your name? (polite/formal)
- **Wie heißt du?** — What is your name? (informal)
- **Danke.** — Thank you.
- **Wie bitte?** — Pardon? / Could you repeat that?

Use **Sie** when addressing someone formally, for example a stranger or teacher unless invited to use **du**. Capitalise formal **Sie**. Use **du** with friends, children, or when people agree to be informal.

**Mini-dialogue**

> A: Guten Tag! Wie heißen Sie?  
> B: Mein Name ist Sara. Und Sie?  
> A: Ich bin Daniel.  
> B: Auf Wiedersehen!

## 3. Alphabet and spelling

The German alphabet has the 26 letters A–Z, plus **ä, ö, ü** and **ß**. The umlaut dots are part of the spelling. **ß** is called *Eszett* or *scharfes S* and sounds like a voiceless “s”; it does not appear at the beginning of a German word.

| Letter | Approximate letter name |
|---|---|
| A, E, I, O, U | ah, eh, ee, oh, oo |
| J | yot |
| W | vay |
| V | fow |
| Z | tset |
| Ä, Ö, Ü | A, O, U with umlaut |

These are approximate English aids: listen to native audio for accurate pronunciation. **ei** usually sounds like English “eye”; **ie** usually sounds like “ee”. In German, **w** usually sounds like English “v”, while **z** sounds like “ts”.

To ask for spelling:

> A: Wie schreibt man das? — How do you spell that?  
> B: Ich buchstabiere: S–A–R–A. — I’ll spell it: S–A–R–A.  
> A: Danke!

Practise saying your own name slowly, letter by letter.

## 4. Countries and where you come from

**Woher kommst du?** — Where are you from? (informal)  
**Woher kommen Sie?** — Where are you from? (formal)  
**Ich komme aus …** — I come from …

| German | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Österreich | Austria | Ich komme aus Österreich. |
| Deutschland | Germany | Ich komme aus Deutschland. |
| Spanien | Spain | Ich komme aus Spanien. |
| Polen | Poland | Ich komme aus Polen. |
| Eritrea | Eritrea | Ich komme aus Eritrea. |
| die Türkei | Turkey | Ich komme **aus der Türkei**. |
| die Slowakei | Slovakia | Ich komme **aus der Slowakei**. |
| der Irak | Iraq | Ich komme **aus dem Irak**. |
| die USA | the United States | Ich komme **aus den USA**. |

Most country names in these examples do not use an article. Some do: learn the whole phrase **aus der Türkei**, **aus dem Irak**, or **aus den USA**. The ending changes after *aus*.

> A: Woher kommst du?  
> B: Ich komme aus Polen. Und du?  
> A: Ich komme aus der Türkei.

## 5. Numbers 0–20

| Number | German | Number | German | Number | German |
|---:|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| 0 | null | 7 | sieben | 14 | vierzehn |
| 1 | eins | 8 | acht | 15 | fünfzehn |
| 2 | zwei | 9 | neun | 16 | sechzehn |
| 3 | drei | 10 | zehn | 17 | siebzehn |
| 4 | vier | 11 | elf | 18 | achtzehn |
| 5 | fünf | 12 | zwölf | 19 | neunzehn |
| 6 | sechs | 13 | dreizehn | 20 | zwanzig |

Notice **sechzehn** and **siebzehn**: they differ from *sechs* and *sieben*. Practise number words aloud and write them from memory.

## 6. Colours

| German | English |
|---|---|
| gelb | yellow |
| rot | red |
| rosa | pink |
| lila | purple |
| blau | blue |
| grün | green |
| braun | brown |
| grau | grey |
| schwarz | black |
| weiß | white |

Ask **Welche Farbe?** — Which colour? Say **Das ist blau.** — That is blue. You can also point to a colour and say its German name.

## 7. Everyday practice

1. Greet someone appropriately for the time of day.
2. Introduce yourself and ask their name.
3. Spell your name; ask them to repeat it with **Wie bitte?**
4. Say where you come from and ask **Und du?** or **Und Sie?**
5. Count objects from 0 to 20 and name three colours you can see.
6. End the conversation with a suitable farewell.

# Practical exercises

Try these without looking at the tables first. Check the answer key afterwards.

## Exercise 1: Choose the phrase

Choose the best answer.

1. You meet your teacher at 8:00.  
   a) Guten Morgen!  b) Gute Nacht!  c) Baba!
2. You leave a formal meeting.  
   a) Auf Wiedersehen!  b) Guten Morgen!  c) Hallo!
3. You say goodbye to a friend in Austria.  
   a) Baba!  b) Guten Abend!  c) Grüß Gott!
4. Someone is going to bed.  
   a) Guten Tag!  b) Gute Nacht!  c) Guten Morgen!
5. You greet someone politely in Austria.  
   a) Grüß Gott!  b) Gute Nacht!  c) Auf Wiedersehen!

## Exercise 2: Complete the conversation

Use **bin**, **Name**, **heißen**, **Wiedersehen**.

> A: Guten Tag! Wie ______ Sie?  
> B: Mein ______ ist Lena. Und Sie?  
> A: Ich ______ Omar.  
> B: Auf ______!

## Exercise 3: Put the words in order

1. bin / Ich / Amir.
2. ist / Name / Mein / Eva.
3. du / heißt / Wie?
4. Sie / heißen / Wie?
5. das / schreibt / man / Wie?

## Exercise 4: Spell and listen

1. Write your first name in capital letters, separated by dashes.
2. Ask a partner to spell their name. Write the letters you hear.
3. Spell these names aloud: **Tariq**, **Anna**, **Daniel**.
4. Say each pair aloud: **ei / ie**, **W / V**, **Ö / O**. Check your pronunciation with the course audio or a reliable dictionary.

## Exercise 5: Countries and origins

Then write two sentences: one about yourself and one about an imaginary person.

Translate into German.

6. yellow   7. pink   8. purple   9. brown   10. grey

Find five objects around you. Write their colours in German.

## Exercise 9: Mixed review

Fill in the blanks.

1. ______ Morgen! (good morning)
2. Ich ______ Maria. (am)
3. Mein ______ ist Samir. (name)
4. Woher ______ du? (come)
5. Ich komme aus ______ Türkei. (the)
6. 12 = ______
7. 17 = ______
8. red = ______
9. “How do you spell that?” = Wie ______ man das?
10. ______ Wiedersehen! (goodbye)

## Exercise 10: Speaking and writing

**A. Partner role-play:** Meet a classmate. Greet them, exchange names, ask where each person comes from, spell one name, and say goodbye. Repeat using formal **Sie**.

**B. Number dictation:** One person says five numbers from 0–20; the other writes numerals. Swap roles and check together.

**C. Colour hunt:** Point to five objects and say the colour of each. Your partner writes the colour words.

**D. Short introduction:** Write four lines using this model, replacing the details:

> Guten Tag!  
> Ich bin ______.  
> Ich komme aus ______.  
> Mein Name: ______ [spell it].
